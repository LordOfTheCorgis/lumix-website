# Brag Plan: Lumix Solutions

## What is this app?
Game server hosting for communities: pick a game, pick a size, it's online in minutes across four US regions, DDoS scrubbed upstream. The twist is the pitch, they cap how many servers they run on purpose instead of overselling.

## The angle
Every host screams "unlimited." Lumix is the one that brags about a limit. The video is a dry, confident infrastructure film that lets the product's own anti-hype voice do the selling: real hero line, real catalog with real prices, the contrarian "capped on purpose" claim, the actual four regions on a globe with real ping. Nothing invented. The swagger is in the restraint.

## Hook (first 2-3 seconds)
The site's real H1 lands hard: **"Order your game server"** in Space Grotesk on warm ink, the red period (`#ff4c4c`) popping on last like a light switch. Overline "GAME SERVERS · FOUR US REGIONS" sits above it. No hype word anywhere. The confidence of a plain instruction is the hook.

## Key moments (the middle)
- The catalog arriving tile by tile with real games and real gold prices: FiveM $8.99, Palworld $9.99, Minecraft $14.99, BeamMP $5.99, Terraria $5.00. Chamfered corners, the site's signature cut.
- The contrarian claim as a deadpan metric slide: **"Capped on purpose."** / "A finite number of servers, run properly."
- The globe/regions beat: four real US cities arriving one by one with red markers (Salt Lake City, Dallas, Ashburn, Miami), a ping counter ticking down to a low millisecond number, "Scrubbed before it reaches you."

## Outro / punchline
**"Online in minutes."** with the red period, the chamfered red "Order now →" button, the Lumix wordmark, and `lumixsolutions.org` · "Plans start at $5/mo. No setup fee." The logo lands on a strong beat with a single bell.

## User flow worth showing
Entry → key action → result maps to: land on the hero (order intent) → pick a game from the catalog grid (the key action) → see where it runs and how fast it connects (the payoff). The catalog grid and the regions/ping beat are the centerpiece scenes, not marketing filler; they're the actual product surface.

## Tone
- Preset: polished
- Creative direction: dry, confident infrastructure film, the anti-hype game host
- Interpretation: fewer scenes, longer holds, restraint over density. The site is genuinely a warm-dark + single-red-accent brand, so that register is earned here, not a default. Motion is one orchestrated system (ember light, red accents, chamfer reveals), never scattered effects.

## Format: landscape — 1920x1080
## Duration: ~20.5 seconds

Most game hosts sell you "unlimited." We built the one that caps its servers on purpose, and runs them properly. Pick a game, it's online in minutes → lumixsolutions.org

- Role: cinematic support, restrained. A clean steady bed with a few dry, motion-matched accents.
- Music: `happy-beats-business-moves-vol-12-by-ende-dot-app.mp3` (steady, clean; suits polished)
- Music treatment: start 0s at volume ~0.30, hold under the whole video, soft fade under the final logo. Never above 0.35.
- Music cue guidance: bundled preset read (tempo ~110 BPM). Lock 2 strong cues: the "Capped on purpose" slam near **8.74s**, the outro logo near **17.47s**. Sequential reveals ride the beat grid: catalog tiles every-other beat (~3.82, 4.91, 6.00, 7.09, 8.19), regions every-other beat (~13.11, 14.20, 15.29, 16.38).
- Audio-reactive treatment: subtle; let the ember light rays and the hero glow breathe with RMS, and the globe markers gain slight presence on bass. No waveform/equalizer graphics, no strobing.
- SFX posture: sparse, motion-matched, polished restraint. Soft impact on the red period, card-place on the first and last catalog tiles, quiet ticks on the ping counter, one bell on the outro logo.
- Audio-coupled moments: the red-period pop, the tile-by-tile catalog, the ping count-down, the region markers, the final logo landing.
- Restraint rule: no sound stacking, no comedic hits, nothing calls attention to itself. If a scene is already carrying motion, let it carry.
